# **⭐ SOP 1 — GENERAL SITE ASSESSMENT (Pools \+ Homes)**

*Cape Plaster & Pools — Internal Operations SOP*

---

## **1\. Purpose**

Establish a clear and consistent process for Matthew and the team to assess any site before work commences.  
This protects us from surprises, controls cost, and ensures clients understand the scope.

---

## **2\. Tools Required**

* Tape measure

* Moisture meter (if interior/exterior plaster)

* Straight edge

* Plumb line/spirit level

* Scraper / small chisel (surface sounding)

* Phone for photos & videos

* Pool depth pole (for pools)

* PPE: gloves, boots, mask

---

## **3\. Safety Notes**

* Check for live electrics (pool pumps, DB boards, extension leads).

* Identify unstable surfaces, loose paving, and sharp tile edges.

* Confirm safe access for team \+ materials.

* Confirm children, pets, or hazards are controlled.

---

## **4\. Assessment Steps**

### **A. Client Briefing**

Short, clear conversation:

* What the client wants

* Problem areas

* Timeline

* Budget awareness

* Materials preference (mosaic vs plaster vs stone)

You take notes. Matthew inspects.

---

### **B. Visual Inspection**

Cover top to bottom:

#### **Pools**

* Check plaster condition (cracks, blisters, spalling).

* Tap-test for hollow spots.

* Look for leaks near weirs, returns, and lights.

* Check mosaic adhesion.

* Inspect coping stones for lift or movement.

* Check structural movement lines.

#### **Interiors**

* Moisture levels.

* Previous coats (PVA, skim, gypsum, cement, paint).

* Cracks: hairline or structural.

* Hollow plaster patches.

* Damp, rising damp, efflorescence.

#### **Exteriors**

* Weather damage.

* Uneven substrates.

* Failed paint.

* Cracks from movement or old repairs.

#### **Paving & Stone**

* Substrate condition (sand, cement bedding).

* Movement gaps.

* Drainage issues.

* Loose pieces.

---

### **C. Substrate Checks**

We check what we will be *building on*, not just what’s visible.

* Scratch test for adhesion.

* Moisture content (high moisture affects plaster curing).

* Waterline damage.

* Algae or deposits in pools.

* Slurry adhesion test for tiles or stone.

---

### **D. Measurements**

Take accurate measurements for:

* m²

* Pool length/width/depth

* Wall height

* Stone lengths

* Paving area including waste %

This determines labour \+ materials.

---

### **E. Photos \+ Video**

You take:

* Wide shots

* Close-ups of damage

* Specific problem areas

* Access points

* Before-condition proof for disputes

Stored in the job folder.

---

### **F. Labour Planning**

Matthew estimates:

* How many guys

* How many days

* Any specialised tasks (stone cutting, mosaic edges, waterproofing)

* Waste removal needs

* Bolt transport needs

---

### **G. Material Planning**

You calculate:

* Bags of plaster

* Cement

* Stone

* Mosaics

* Grout

* Waterproofing

* Bonding liquids

* Acid wash chemicals

* Sand, aggregates

Built into the quote.

---

### **H. Risk & Cost Flags**

You identify:

* Structural cracks (quote higher)

* Water leaks (must fix first)

* High moisture (delay plaster day)

* Weak substrate (extra prep)

* Access difficulty (extra time)

This prevents underquoting.

---

## **5\. Final Client Discussion**

Clear, straightforward:

* Scope of work

* Timeline

* Deposit required

* Weather delays possible

* Colour/material choices

* Warranty terms

* Aftercare expectations

You send the final quote.

---

## **6\. Outcome**

A clean, well-planned job.  
 No surprises.  
 Clients trust the process.  
 Your crew knows exactly what they’re walking into.

# ⭐ SOP 2 — POOL PLASTERING WORKFLOW

# **⭐ SOP 2 — POOL PLASTERING WORKFLOW**

*Cape Plaster & Pools — Internal Operations SOP*

This SOP covers how our team performs a full pool replaster or new plaster installation using correct South African materials, tools, and standards.

---

# **1\. Purpose**

Create a consistent, high-quality pool plaster finish that cures well, lasts long, and avoids cracking, mottling, or delamination.

---

# **2\. Tools Required**

* Trowels (pool trowel, finishing trowel)

* Steel floats

* Rubber float (for final smoothing if needed)

* Buckets

* Mixing drums

* Power mixer

* Acids \+ neutraliser

* Stiff brush \+ pool broom

* Sponge

* Wheelbarrow

* Protective gear: gloves, boots, safety glasses

* Hosepipe with a clean water supply

* Plaster hawk (for mixing-on-the-go)

---

# **3\. Materials**

* Pool plaster mix (white cement \+ marble dust OR prebagged quartz/colour plaster)

* Bonding liquid/pool slurry (cement \+ bonding agent)

* Mosaic tiles (if applicable)

* Grout (non-shrink pool-grade)

* Acid wash solution (hydrochloric acid diluted correctly)

* Neutraliser (bicarbonate soda)

* Masking tape / plastic sheets

---

# **4\. Safety Notes**

* Acid washes must be done with full PPE.

* Never mix acid with water incorrectly — always add acid **to** water, not the other way around.

* Keep children and pets away.

* Ensure pump and DB board electrics are safe.

* Ensure proper ventilation when working with bonding agents.

---

# **5\. Step-by-Step Method**

---

## **A. Drain the Pool**

1. Use a pump or gravity to drain fully.

2. Remove debris and sediment.

3. Inspect for cracks, hollow areas, blisters, and chalking.

*(This links back to SOP 1 assessment.)*

---

## **B. Surface Prep**

This step determines whether the plaster will last.

### **1\. Acid Wash**

* Light acid wash to remove algae, minerals, and old plaster dust.

* Scrub with a stiff brush.

* Rinse thoroughly.

* Neutralise properly.

### **2\. Chip Back Loose Areas**

* Tap-test the entire pool.

* Chip hollow or blistering sections back to the solid substrate.

* Feather edges cleanly.

### **3\. Repair Cracks**

* Widen cracks slightly (V-shape) to allow bonding.

* Fill with cement-based repair mix or epoxy-based underwater-approved filler.

### **4\. Final Clean**

* Rinse again.

* The pool must be dust-free.

* No loose debris.

---

## **C. Apply Bonding Slurry**

This ensures the new plaster adheres.

### **Mix:**

* Cement \+ bonding liquid \+ water

* Smooth brushable texture

### **Apply:**

* Brush onto walls and floor.

* No missed spots — must be even.

* Let it go tacky (not dry).

This creates the perfect chemical “grab” for the new plaster.

---

## **D. Mix the Plaster**

Consistency is key.

### **Standard ratio (for white marbelite-style plaster):**

* 1 part white cement

* 1.5–2 parts marble dust

### **For coloured quartz plaster:**

* Follow the manufacturer’s exact ratios (Bag A \+ Bag B system)

### **Notes:**

* Mix in batches to avoid drying differences.

* Never overwater — leads to softness and streaking.

* Keep batches consistent in colour.

---

## **E. Apply the Plaster**

### **1\. Start at the deep end floor**

* Use trowels to apply an even layer ±6–10mm thick.

* Smooth immediately.

### **2\. Move up the walls**

* Work fast; you don’t want joins.

* Maintain a wet edge.

* Keep motion consistent.

### **3\. Maintain even thickness**

* No lumps, ridges, or dry patches.

* The team must work in sync.

### **4\. Final smoothing**

* Light passes with a clean trowel.

* Avoid over-troweling (burnishing).

### **5\. Check corners \+ steps**

* Smooth transitions.

* Uniform thickness.

---

## **F. Finishing \+ Curing**

### **1\. Brush Finish (if required)**

Some finishes need a light brush to expose quartz.

### **2\. Start filling immediately**

* Fill from a clean hose.

* NO STOPPING until the water reaches the middle of the waterline.

Stopping causes ring marks.

### **3\. Brush the pool twice daily for one week**

This removes plaster dust and helps cure the surface.

### **4\. Chemical Startup**

* No chlorine for the first 3–5 days.

* Balance pH gently.

* Avoid shocking the pool for 30 days.

---

# **6\. Common Mistakes to Avoid**

* Starting plaster on dry slurry

* Overmixing (causes rapid setting)

* Adding too much water

* Working too slowly

* Letting the hose touch the fresh plaster

* Delayed filling

* High chlorine too early

* Not brushing daily

---

# **7\. Quality Standard**

A Cape Plaster & Pools plaster job must always be:

* Smooth

* Even colour

* No bumps or hollows

* No trowel burns

* No cold joints

* Proper curing marks (slight colour variation is normal early on)

* Fully bonded with no hollow sounds

---

# **8\. Client Handover**

* Explain the curing period.

* Give brushing instructions.

* Explain a chemical startup.

* Provide warranty conditions.

* Set expectation: plaster colour matures over 30 days.

# ⭐ SOP 3 — POOL MOSAIC INSTALLATION

# **⭐ SOP 3 — POOL MOSAIC INSTALLATION**

*Cape Plaster & Pools — Internal Operations SOP*

This is your complete and realistic workflow for installing mosaics inside a pool — the right tools, the right methods, and absolutely zero “fake” techniques.  
 This is the gold standard your team should follow.

---

# **1\. Purpose**

Install pool mosaics safely and correctly so they adhere fully, follow the pool’s curves, stay aligned, and look high-end.

---

# **2\. Tools Required**

* Rubber grout float (for pressing the sheets)

* Plastic tile spacers

* Margin trowel (only for adhesive — NEVER used on tile sheets)

* Notched trowel (3mm–4mm for mosaic)

* Buckets

* Sponge

* Clean cloth

* Rubber mallet (gentle tapping only)

* Tile sheet cutter or scissors (for mesh)

* Level or straight edge

* Laser line (optional for waterline straightness)

* Protective gloves

---

# **3\. Materials**

* Pool-grade thinset adhesive

* Pool-grade grout (non-shrink, waterproof)

* Mosaic tile sheets (glass, ceramic, or porcelain)

* Bonding liquid (if applying over repaired areas)

* Water \+ sponge buckets

---

# **4\. Safety Notes**

* Keep tiles off the floor where people walk — slippery hazard.

* Never let the tile sheet touch the fresh plaster unless intentional.

* No sharp knives near mosaic sheets (can cut the mesh).

* Ensure pool pump electrics are isolated.

---

# **5\. Step-by-Step Method**

---

## **A. Surface Preparation**

1. Surface must be solid, clean, and dust-free.

2. Remove loose plaster or tile.

3. Smooth out uneven patches.

4. If the surface has been repaired, apply a bonding slurry first.

5. Let the slurry go tacky — not dry.

---

## **B. Apply Adhesive (Correctly)**

### **1\. Mix pool-grade thinset**

Should be:

* Smooth

* Sticky

* Not watery

### **2\. Use a notched trowel**

* Spread a thin, even layer on the pool wall.

* Maintain consistent grooves.

### **❌ NEVER apply the notched trowel to the tile sheet.**

---

## **C. Place the Mosaic Sheet**

### **1\. Hold the sheet with both hands**

Let it naturally flex — mosaic sheets bend to follow curves.

### **2\. Align the sheet to your laser line or guide string**

This keeps lines straight across curved walls.

### **3\. Press the sheet onto the adhesive**

Use:

* **Hands** first

* Then **rubber grout floats** to press evenly.

Never use metal tools.

---

## **D. Adjust \+ Join Sheets**

### **1\. Match joins carefully**

Check:

* Alignment

* Gaps

* Curvature

* Tile spacing

### **2\. Trim mesh if needed**

Scissors only.  
 Never snap tiles unless required.

### **3\. Light tapping with rubber mallet (optional)**

To ensure full contact — but always gentle.

---

## **E. Check Adhesion**

* Lift one corner *slightly* to confirm adhesive contact.

* If tiles fall, the adhesive was too dry or too thin.

* Reset and reapply adhesive.

---

## **F. Allow to Set**

Let the adhesive cure for 12–24 hours before grouting.  
 Do **not** grout too early — it weakens adhesion.

---

## **G. Grouting**

### **1\. Mix pool-grade grout**

Smooth, workable, not watery.

### **2\. Apply with a rubber grout float**

Swipe diagonally across tile lines.

### **3\. Pack grout fully into joints**

Avoid hollow areas or missed spots.

### **4\. Sponge clean**

Lightly wipe the surface to remove excess grout.  
 Rinse the sponge often.

### **5\. Final polish with a dry cloth**

Once the haze disappears.

---

# **6\. Common Mistakes to Avoid**

❌ Using a metal trowel on the tile sheet  
 ❌ Not applying enough adhesive  
 ❌ Uneven tile spacing  
 ❌ Letting adhesive skin over before placing  
 ❌ Using non-pool grout (it cracks)  
 ❌ Allowing mosaic to slip on steep walls (use temporary support if needed)  
 ❌ Applying grout while the adhesive is still wet

---

# **7\. Quality Standard**

A Cape Plaster & Pools mosaic installation must be:

* Even

* Aligned

* No tiles protruding

* No mesh visible

* Smooth curvature

* Correct spacing

* Fully bonded

This is what gives the work that luxury, resort-level finish.

---

# **8\. Client Handover**

* Explain the curing time before pool filling.

* Show them the mosaic work.

* Explain why brushing and chemical startup still matter.

* Provide care instructions.

# ⭐ SOP 4 — POOL REPAIR & RESURFACING

# **⭐ SOP 4 — POOL REPAIR & RESURFACING**

*Cape Plaster & Pools — Internal Operations SOP*

This SOP covers every type of repair we do on existing pools before resurfacing or replastering. This is the backbone of long-lasting quality — resurfacing only looks good when the prep work is correct.

---

# **1\. Purpose**

Repair structural and cosmetic damage inside a pool so that the new plaster or mosaic layer bonds correctly, stays even, and does not fail prematurely.

---

# **2\. Types of Pool Repairs Covered**

### **✔ Structural cracks**

### **✔ Blisters/delamination**

### **✔ Spalling plaster**

### **✔ Hollow spots (drummy areas)**

### **✔ Rust spots**

### **✔ Mosaic failure**

### **✔ Waterline tile damage**

### **✔ Skimmer leaks**

### **✔ Light niche repair**

### **✔ Return jet leaks**

### **✔ Step edge crumbling**

### **✔ Coping loosening**

Each repair type has a different method.

---

# **3\. Tools Needed**

* Angle grinder

* Chisel \+ hammer

* Crack chaser blade

* Margin trowel

* Pool trowel

* Wire brush

* Scraper

* Epoxy filler tools

* Buckets

* Mixing drill

* Acid wash kit

* PPE: gloves, goggles, mask

* Sponge \+ hose

---

# **4\. Materials Needed**

* Cement-based repair mortar

* Pool-grade epoxy filler

* Waterproofing slurry (cementitious)

* Bonding liquid

* Plaster mix (for patching)

* Mosaic tiles (if needed)

* Pool grout

* Anti-rust primer

* Sealant compatible with pool environments

---

# **5\. Step-by-Step Repair Methods**

---

## **A. Blisters / Hollow Spots / Drummy Areas**

These are the most common failures in old pools.

### **1\. Tap-test entire surface**

Mark hollow spots.

### **2\. Chip away loose plaster**

* Feather the edges

* Remove all weak areas.

* Do NOT leave a hollow pocket.s

### **3\. Clean the area**

Remove dust, debris, and algae.

### **4\. Apply bonding slurry**

Let it go tacky.

### **5\. Patch with plaster mix**

Match thickness  
 Smooth trowel finish  
 Allow drying time

---

## **B. Structural Cracks**

These are movement cracks — must be repaired correctly.

### **1\. V-cut the crack**

Use a grinder or crack chaser.  
 Creates space for fill.

### **2\. Clean thoroughly**

No dust or debris inside.

### **3\. Apply epoxy filler**

Best for movement cracks.

### **4\. Feather plaster over the crack**

Blend into the surface, smooth.

### **5\. Cure properly**

Avoid moisture in cracks during drying.

---

## **C. Rust Spots**

Rust leaches through plaster and stains forever unless repaired.

### **1\. Chip back to source**

Expose rebar or metal tie.

### **2\. Clean rust off metal**

Wire brush thoroughly.

### **3\. Apply anti-rust primer**

Fully coat the metal.

### **4\. Rebuild the area**

Bonding slurry → patch → smooth finish.

### **2\. Apply keycoat if needed**

### **3\. Repair cracks**

### **4\. Mask off**

Protect:

* Floors

* Lights

* Sockets

* Windows

---

## **C. Mixing the Plaster**

### **Standard interior plaster mix:**

* 1 part cement

* 4–5 parts plaster sand

* Water to a workable consistency

### **Key principles:**

* Mix small batches

* No overwatering

* Consistency must stay even across the room.

---

## 

## **D. Application — Walls**

### **1\. First Coat (Scratch Coat)**

* 8–12 mm thick

* Applied firmly

* Scratch horizontal lines for the mechanical key

### **2\. Second Coat**

* 3–5 mm

* Smooth and uniform

* Use a straight edge to level.

* Keep edges tight

### **3\. Final Trowel**

* Light, clean passes

* Don’t overwet the surface.e

* Avoid over-troweling (causes shine spots)

### **4\. Corners \+ Edges**

Use a corner trowel for crisp lines.

---

## **E. Application — Ceilings**

Ceilings require more speed and support.

### **1\. Secure substrate**

Check:

* No sagging

* No hollow boards

* No failing skim

### **2\. Apply plaster quickly**

Gravity works against you.

### **3\. Use wider trowel strokes**

Smooth evenly.

### **4\. Blend edges**

Ensure uniform appearance with walls.

---

## **F. Gypsum Skim (Optional Finisher)**

Used for ultra-smooth finishes.

### **1\. Apply a thin layer**

1–2mm maximum.

### **2\. Smooth quickly**

Gypsum sets fast.

### **3\. Final polish**

Light touches — don’t overwork.

---

# **6\. Drying \+ Curing**

* Allow 24–48 hours before priming (PVA).

* Avoid fans or heaters blowing directly on fresh plaster.

* Full cure \~7 days depending on thickness.

# **4\. Safety Notes**

# **5\. Step-by-Step Method**

---

## **A. Surface Preparation**

1. Clean ceiling \+ wall contact surfaces

2. Remove dust, loose paint, and cobwebs.

3. Repair cracks in the area first

4. For very smooth surfaces:

   * Apply bonding liquid

   * Or lightly roughen with sandpaper.

This ensures the cornice adhesive grips.

---

## **B. Measurement & Marking**

1. Measure room perimeter

2. Snap a chalk line (optional but helpful for long runs)

3. Check ceiling level — older houses may have a dip.s

4. Mark mitre angles:

   * **Internal corners:** 45° inward

   * **External corners:** 45° outward

Accurate marking \= perfect joints.

---

## **C. Cutting the Cornice**

1. Place the cornice in the mitre box the correct way up.

2. Use a fine-tooth saw for a clean cut.

3. Test-fit before applying adhesive

4. Sand very lightly if needed for flush joints

### **2\. Use exterior-grade adhesive \+ grout**

Weather-resistant.

### **3\. Expansion joints are mandatory**

Heat causes expansion.

### **4\. Full bedding for large tiles**

No voids — prevents cracks.

❌ Not waterproofing wet areas  
 ❌ Uneven adhesive coverage  
 ❌ Hollow spots under tiles  
 ❌ Crooked grout lines  
 ❌ Silver cut edges at borders  
 ❌ Grouting too early  
 ❌ No expansion joints  
 ❌ Using cheap adhesive (tiles will pop)

# **⭐ 2\. AFTERCARE — POOLS**

### **Day 0 — Filling**

* Pool **must be filled immediately** after plastering

* Hose placed at the deepest step

* No moving the hose mid-fill

* No stopping the fill

* Do NOT drop the hose (it leaves marks)

Interrupting fill → permanent ring.

---

## **B. First 14 Days**

* Brush pool **twice a day**

* Use soft-bristle brush

* No swimming

* No vacuuming

* No chemicals except stabiliser (if needed)

* No pool cleaner

* No salt

* No heat pump

* No shocking

The goal \= prevent streaking, mottling, or early blemishes.

---

## **C. Day 14–30**

* Add chemicals slowly

* Add salt last

* Run pump daily

* Keep pH stable

* Swim after 21 days if water is balanced

Avoid heavy pool use early.

---

## **D. Long-Term Care**

* Keep pH between 7.2–7.6

* Brush once a week

* Avoid metal objects in pool

* Don’t let leaves rot on the surface

* Clean mosaics with a soft pad
